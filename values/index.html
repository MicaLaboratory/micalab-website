mica lab values

These are the values that guide how we work in the mica lab. Tom Mitchell, who convenes the lab, wrote this first draft, and everyone in the lab will help shape them from here.

Read why Tom wrote them

  1. 1. Care

    Look after yourself and each other. Care cuts across everything we do and is the shared, everyday practice of keeping one another, and the things we make, going. Working patterns can become unsustainable and external pressures can take whatever you can give, but we persist through collaborative survival (Tsing, 2015). Importantly, no-one should lose sleep over work: when a deadline and someone's wellbeing are in tension, we move the deadline or share the load.

  2. 2. Assemblages

    Individualism is an illusion. Our work is an assemblage of people, bodies, tools and materials, and agency is distributed (Bennett, 2010). No part works in service to another; each shapes, and is shaped by, the rest. We are interdependent: we need one another, even when what we each bring is different, and there is nothing to hide in that (Sins Invalid, 2019). Attributing credit is part of caring for one another.

  3. 3. Decentring

    The normative Western, non-disabled male has long sat above a hierarchy of otherness (Braidotti, 2013), and too often he is the body our technologies are designed around. Push back against that hierarchy where you can. Acknowledge and share your privilege to lift others up. Let everybody have their say, in whatever form that takes. Work with people because you trust them, and don't chase status or what people can do for you. Trust takes time to build, and that time is worth giving.

  4. 4. Becoming-with

    Meet others with respect and curiosity, and be changed by them (Haraway, 2008). Disabled people are collaborators, makers and knowers, not users to be designed for (Hamraie and Fritsch, 2019). Access is not an afterthought; it is where the real work begins.

  5. 5. Diffraction

    Difference is generative. Diffraction attends to the differences that matter, rather than reflecting more of the same (Barad, 2007). Meeting others with care widens what we know and reduces our ignorance, but what people bring is never a resource to be extracted.

  6. 6. Situatedness

    There is no neutral view from nowhere; every judgement is made from somewhere (Haraway, 1988). People are always becoming, never fixed, so don't pass judgement on them (Deleuze, 1997). Speak instead to what was done and what it did. When you are asked to judge work, be open that yours is one view among many.

  7. 7. Affirmation

    Find joy in finding things out, and protect the time and space to do it. Critique can wear us down. Affirmation acknowledges what's wrong and works towards making something better (Braidotti, 2019). Build what you want to see.

  8. 8. Response-ability

    If something opposes what's above, feel free to say no. Answer for what we make: where its materials come from, and where they end up (Haraway, 2016). If you think Tom, or anyone else in the lab, falls short, please say so. You can tell Tom directly or Larry Bull, Tom's line manager.

Tom brings the mica lab together.

Tom wrote these values.

Values are ideas about how we should act.

Everyone in the lab will help change them.

You can read why Tom wrote them.

  1. 1. Care

    We look after ourselves.

    We look after each other.

    Care is part of everything we do.

    Work should never stop you sleeping.

    A deadline is the date work must be finished by.

    If work is too much, we will move the deadline.

    Or we will share the work.

  2. 2. Working together

    Nobody does good work alone.

    People, tools and things all help make our work.

    Everyone's part matters.

    It is OK to need help.

    We always say who helped.

  3. 3. Fairness

    Some people are treated as more important than others.

    Technology is often made for them and not for everyone else.

    This is not fair.

    We work to change this.

    If you have more chances in life, use them to help others.

    Everyone gets a say.

    People can share ideas in any way that works for them.

    We work with people we trust.

    We do not work with people just because they are important.

    Trust takes time.

  4. 4. Disabled people lead

    Disabled people make things with us.

    Disabled people know things we need to learn.

    Access means making sure everyone can take part.

    We think about access first, not last.

  5. 5. Learning from difference

    People are different.

    Differences help us learn new things.

    Meeting people with care helps us understand more.

    We never use people's differences to get things for ourselves.

  6. 6. Not judging people

    Everyone sees things from where they stand.

    People can change.

    We do not judge people.

    We talk about what someone did and what happened.

    If someone asks what we think of their work, we say it is just our view.

  7. 7. Joy

    Finding things out should be fun.

    We make time for this.

    Only talking about what is wrong can wear us down.

    We see what is wrong.

    Then we work together to make it better.

  8. 8. Speaking up

    You can say no to things that go against these values.

    We think about where our materials come from.

    We think about what happens to them after.

    These values are for everyone in the lab.

    That includes Tom.

    If someone does not follow these values, please say so.

    You can tell Tom.

    Or you can tell Larry Bull.

    Larry is Tom's manager.
